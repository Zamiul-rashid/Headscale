The problem

Slots exist only on paper: anyone with a PC’s AnyDesk details can connect

At any time

AnyDesk connects through AnyDesk’s own servers. A PC’s ID and password work outside the slot too.

Into anyone’s work

They can stop another group’s training run, change its files or take over its desktop.

With no record

Nobody can tell afterwards who connected, or when.

Today each thesis group has two slots of about 12 hours a week on a lab PC, with one to five students, often on several devices each.

Tailscale pricing: tailscale.com/pricing (October 2026)

5

We compared the realistic options. Port forwarding exposes SSH to the internet. Plain WireGuard means managing keys by hand and pushing everything through one hub. Hosted Tailscale works well but costs per user beyond six and keeps the control and user data off campus. NetBird is capable but heavier to run. Headscale is one program with SQLite, uses the official Tailscale apps students may already have, is free, and its access policy is one document we can regenerate at every slot change. That last point is why we chose it.

How it works · a connection

A connection goes direct when it can, and through our own relay when it cannot

STEP 1

Sign in, get peers

Each device keeps an HTTPS session to Headscale and gets only the peers the policy allows.

STEP 2

Start on our relay

A new connection starts through our relay (DERP) while STUN finds each end’s public address.

STEP 3

Go direct if possible

If a direct UDP path works, traffic moves to it. If UDP is blocked, it stays relayed over TCP 443.

ALWAYS

Encrypted end to end

Every path is WireGuard. The relay only ever sees ciphertext; Headscale never sees traffic.

Our relay runs on the lab server, so relayed traffic stays on campus.

Off campus with only TCP 443 open, the rehearsal still connected at 27–37 ms.

After Tailscale, “How NAT traversal works” (2020)

8

How a student's laptop actually reaches the PC. The device signs in and learns which peers it may talk to. The connection starts through our own relay, so it works at once, and both ends meanwhile try to punch a direct path through their NATs. If that works, traffic moves to the direct path; on networks that block UDP it stays on the relay over port 443, slower but working. Either way it is WireGuard encrypted end to end. Because the relay is on our server, nothing goes through a third party.

9

Admins keep doing what they do today: picking a group's code in the Sheet. The website reads the Sheet every 30 seconds and checks every cell; a bad cell keeps its last good value and is reported. The slot agent runs every 15 seconds. Slots run 06:00 to 17:55 and 18:00 to 05:55. At 17:55 the slot is closed first: the policy no longer lets the outgoing group in, and on each PC its sessions and processes are ended. For five minutes nobody but the admins can reach the PC. At 18:00 the incoming group is granted and its account becomes the only active one. If anything fails, the agent loads an admins-only policy. No policy at all would mean open access, so failure always means closed.

10

This is the access model the policy encodes. Admins can reach every PC. The slot agent can reach every PC but only log in as its own service account, labsched, which can do one thing: switch the active account. A group in its slot reaches its own PC and logs in as its group's account. Everyone else, including the same group outside its slot, reaches nothing. Logins use Tailscale SSH, which checks the caller's tailnet identity, so there are no SSH keys or passwords to distribute or revoke.

The pilot needs little from IT: one IP address, one domain name, two ports and a lab email account.

Appendix B · remote desktop

AnyDesk goes around the policy, so remote desktop moves onto the tailnet

Today: AnyDesk

Connects through AnyDesk’s own servers, not our tailnet, so our policy cannot control it. Its free licence does not cover lab use.

Planned: over the tailnet

Unattended AnyDesk turned off on enrolled PCs. RustDesk in direct mode, or xrdp, on a port added to the group’s slot.

Status: designed, not built or tested yet. The same policy will then control remote desktop exactly as it controls SSH.
